`Client.connect` now gives up on a server that doesn't answer, such as one that accepts the connection but never completes the TLS handshake, after `Options.rpc_timeout` (30 s without one), returning `error.ConnectFailed` with the reason logged; it used to wait forever. While 4 runtimes of attempts that gave up are kept in the process's pool (see below) and none matches, `connect` fails at once instead of creating another.
A client from `Client.init` keeps at most 4 connection attempts in flight against such a server, where it used to start another with every call.
Closing a client, or a `connect` that gave up, while sdk-core still makes a connection attempt no longer leaves its runtime, threads and log forwarding allocated for the rest of the process: the runtime is kept in a pool and freed once the attempt ends, and a later `Client.init` or `Client.connect` with the same options takes it and waits on that attempt instead of starting another. Before, after 32 such clients with sdk-core logging on, every new client failed with `error.RuntimeInitFailed`.
